## Methodology correction discovered after Step 10

The Baseline v1 probe defines target regions using the repository's coarse semantic mapping. In that mapping, `left_ankle` targets the entire `left_leg` region, which includes `left_hip`, `left_knee`, `left_ankle`, and `left_foot`.

During Steps 6–10 of today's secondary analysis, the ankle-specific helper code incorrectly narrowed the target set to only `left_ankle` + `left_foot`. As a result, `left_knee` and `left_hip` were incorrectly counted as ankle "outside" displacement in those secondary tables.

This inconsistency affects **ankle-only interpretations in Steps 6–10**. It does **not** affect the wrist analysis (`left_wrist` + `left_hand`) or shoulder analysis (`left_shoulder` + `left_elbow` + `left_wrist` + `left_hand`), which match the Baseline v1 coarse-region definitions.

Therefore:

- prior claims that ankle outside displacement is dominated by `left_knee` are **superseded pending recomputation**,
- the Baseline v1 headline ankle leakage ratio of 26.57% remains valid because it was computed with the correct `left_leg` region,
- wrist and shoulder findings below remain valid,
- all ankle rows in the archived Step 7/9/10 secondary CSVs should be treated as provisional until corrected values are generated.

## Current interpretation

The valid wrist results argue strongly against random numerical noise, a few isolated outliers, or a sign-specific instability. The wrist residual behaves like a deterministic, approximately linear coupling:

1. residual displacement scales almost linearly with perturbation magnitude,
2. positive and negative rotations are nearly mirror-symmetric,
3. leakage ratio is almost constant over a six-fold change in angle magnitude,
4. upstream and contralateral anatomical proportions are nearly invariant.

However, this is **not yet evidence of a HUGS-specific representation failure**. HUGS uses blended SMPL/LBS transforms based on neighboring vertices rather than the single dominant-joint labels used by the diagnostic.

## Critical caveats

- one NeuMan sequence,
- one frame,
- primary spatial analysis focused on z-axis perturbations,
- dominant-joint assignment is a diagnostic grouping, not ground-truth segmentation,
- HUGS applies blended skinning transforms, not single-joint transforms,
- ankle secondary analyses from Steps 6–10 used an inconsistent narrow target mask and must be corrected,
- no causal claim should yet be made about Gaussian representation error.
