## Important metric-definition clarification

Two different locality definitions had been mixed together in the earlier interpretation.

The HUGS probe code defines `left_ankle` against the coarse semantic region `left_leg`:

```text
left_hip + left_knee + left_ankle + left_foot
```

By contrast, the archived `joint_axis_leakage.csv` ankle row reports `num_target = 16730`. The semantic assignment counts show that `left_ankle = 13369` and `left_foot = 3361`, which sum exactly to 16730. Therefore the 26.57% ankle value in that table corresponds to a stricter fine-grained ankle/foot target, not the full-left-leg region used by the probe's coarse semantic target.

These are both useful diagnostics, but they answer different questions:

- **fine joint-local leakage:** how much displacement leaves ankle/foot, including propagation into knee/hip,
- **coarse regional leakage:** how much displacement leaves the entire ipsilateral leg.

The earlier statement that one of these definitions was simply "wrong" is therefore replaced by this multi-scale interpretation.

## Step 1–6 — Spatial localization

### Fine ankle/foot diagnostic

Using the stricter ankle/foot target, the apparent ankle z leakage is 26.57%. Much of its outside displacement is assigned to the left knee. This should be interpreted as strong intra-leg propagation under a fine locality definition, not automatically as global deformation leakage.

### Wrist z

The wrist diagnostic remains directly useful because the target semantic region `left_hand` contains the dominant `left_wrist` and `left_hand` assignments.

Main outside sources include:

- left elbow = 39.57%
- right wrist = 12.05%
- left shoulder = 11.26%
- right elbow = 9.93%
- right shoulder = 9.12%
- right hand = 5.58%

### Shoulder z

Overall shoulder leakage is very small. Its outside composition includes collar/spine and contralateral upper-limb assignments, but composition percentages must be interpreted together with the tiny absolute leakage magnitude.

- ±5° -> high-confidence outside displacement ~5.083

The high-confidence leakage ratio remains essentially constant near 12.26%, with upstream same-side share near 54.5% and contralateral share near 41.0%.

This strongly suggests deterministic coupling rather than random numerical noise or isolated outliers.

## Main interpretation today

The ankle results are best described hierarchically rather than with one binary leakage number.

The strict ankle/foot metric reports large apparent leakage because it counts same-leg knee/hip response as outside. When the target expands to the complete ipsilateral leg, leakage falls from the fine-scale 26.57% figure to about 5.38%, and to about 3.13% after confidence filtering.

This suggests that a large portion of the ankle response is **intra-limb coupling**, while a smaller but measurable residual crosses the limb boundary.

The wrist remains the more interesting non-local case: its high-confidence response is linear, sign-symmetric, and retains a stable contralateral component across perturbation magnitude.

## Critical caveats

- one NeuMan sequence,
- one frame,
- primary spatial analysis focused on z-axis perturbations,
- dominant-joint assignment is a diagnostic grouping rather than ground-truth segmentation,
- HUGS applies blended SMPL/LBS transforms rather than single-joint transforms,
- fine and coarse locality definitions must be reported separately,
- no causal claim should yet be made about Gaussian representation error.

## Next analysis

1. intended joint/part,

3. genuinely non-local / cross-body remainder.

This will put ankle, wrist, and shoulder on the same interpretable scale. After that, run an SMPL-only control to determine how much of the residual already exists in the underlying body/skinning model.
