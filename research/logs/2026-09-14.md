# 2026-09-14 — Spatial Leakage Localization

## Research question

Where are the non-target Gaussians responsible for the large distal-joint leakage observed in HUGS Baseline v1, and what mechanism best explains that displacement?

## Starting point

Yesterday's Baseline v1 established a strong articulation-dependent pattern on NeuMan Seattle, frame 0:

- left ankle z: 26.57% leakage
- left wrist z: 11.21% leakage
- left shoulder z: 0.58% leakage

The goal today is not to add another headline number. It is to explain the spatial structure behind those numbers.

## Findings from Steps 1–6

### Left ankle z

The ankle probe is highly concentrated rather than uniformly distributed across the body.

- left knee accounts for **78.08% of all outside displacement**
- right knee: 4.83%
- right ankle: 3.53%
- every other listed joint contributes less than 3%

This strongly suggests that the large 26.57% ankle leakage is dominated by a specific anatomically adjacent upstream region, especially the left-knee assignment, rather than generic global drift.

The mean dominant-joint confidence for the left-knee group is 0.842, so the effect is not obviously explained by only very low-confidence semantic assignments.

### Left wrist z

The wrist probe is more distributed than the ankle probe.

- left elbow: **39.57% of outside displacement**
- right wrist: 12.05%
- left shoulder: 11.26%
- right elbow: 9.93%
- right shoulder: 9.12%
- right hand: 5.58%

The largest component is again an anatomically adjacent upstream joint, the left elbow. However, a substantial fraction also appears on the contralateral arm. This makes the wrist result qualitatively different from the ankle result and motivates checking whether the contralateral displacement is a real deformation effect, a consequence of Gaussian-to-joint assignment, or a coordinate/pose coupling effect.

### Left shoulder z

The shoulder probe has a very small overall leakage ratio, 0.58%. Its outside-displacement composition should therefore not be compared directly with the ankle or wrist totals.

Within that small outside component:

- left collar: 21.48%
- spine3: 20.32%
- right elbow: 14.12%
- right wrist: 11.16%

The strongest assignments to left collar and spine3 are anatomically plausible neighboring regions for shoulder motion. This supports the interpretation that proximal-joint leakage is both lower in magnitude and more concentrated around expected torso/shoulder-chain boundaries.

## Spatial visualization observations

The highest-displacement non-target Gaussian visualizations were inspected for ankle z, wrist z, and shoulder z.

- **ankle z:** the displacement scale is much larger than for wrist or shoulder, with a small number of extreme high-displacement points and a broader affected structure along the posed body.
- **wrist z:** the highest non-target points form clearer clusters around the upper-limb / torso structure, consistent with the left-elbow and shoulder contributions in the joint breakdown.
- **shoulder z:** the non-target points are comparatively low magnitude and concentrate around adjacent torso / shoulder-chain regions, although isolated distant outliers remain visible.

These plots show only the selected highest-displacement non-target Gaussians and therefore should not be interpreted as a density map of all leakage.

### Anatomical grouping

For ankle z, the upstream same-side group accounts for **81.31%** of outside displacement. The contralateral leg contributes 9.99%, torso/head 7.30%, and upper-body limbs 1.40%.

For wrist z, the upstream same-side group contributes **52.80%**, while the contralateral upper limb contributes a substantial **36.99%**. Legs contribute 6.71% and torso/head 3.51%.

For shoulder z, the upstream torso attachment contributes **47.20%**, the contralateral upper limb **38.70%**, legs 13.74%, and head 0.36%.

### Displacement concentration

The displacement distribution is strongly heavy-tailed across all three probes:

| Probe | Top 0.1% | Top 1% | Top 5% | Top 10% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ankle z | 6.24% | 32.65% | 82.69% | 93.49% |
| wrist z | 9.87% | 44.71% | 83.75% | 94.78% |
| shoulder z | 13.42% | 50.53% | 85.44% | 94.46% |

This rules out a simple explanation in which only a few isolated extreme Gaussians inflate the leakage metric. The top 0.1% explain only 6–13% of outside displacement, while a broader top 5% subset consistently explains about 83–85%. The leakage is therefore concentrated, but it is a structured high-displacement subset rather than a handful of single-point outliers.

A useful secondary observation is that the concentration shape is surprisingly similar across ankle, wrist, and shoulder despite their very different absolute leakage ratios. This suggests that **leakage magnitude** and **leakage concentration shape** may be partly separable phenomena.

## Important implementation note discovered during analysis

HUGS does not deform each Gaussian using the single dominant joint used by our diagnostic labels. In the upstream implementation, the non-deformer path estimates each Gaussian's transform from a K=6 neighborhood of SMPL template vertices and blends their transforms according to distance and similarity of their LBS-weight vectors. Therefore, our nearest-vertex dominant-joint label is useful for interpretation but is not the actual deformation rule used by HUGS.

This matters because part of what we currently call "outside displacement" may be expected multi-joint skinning influence rather than a representation-specific locality failure. The next analyses must explicitly test this possibility before making a locality-failure claim.

## Current interpretation

The current evidence weakens both a random-global-drift explanation and a single-outlier explanation. Instead:

1. distal ankle motion produces the largest overall leakage and is dominated by an upstream same-side anatomical region,
2. wrist motion is dominated by its upstream chain but also contains a large contralateral upper-limb component,
3. shoulder motion has very low overall leakage but a similarly concentrated high-displacement tail,
4. across all probes, roughly the top 5% of non-target Gaussians account for more than 82% of outside displacement,
5. because HUGS uses blended KNN/LBS transforms rather than our dominant-joint diagnostic labels, the current metric may over-count expected blended deformation as leakage.

## Important caveats

- one NeuMan sequence,
- one frame,
- z-axis comparison for the primary spatial analysis,
- dominant-joint assignment is a diagnostic grouping rather than HUGS's actual deformation weighting,
- the current 3D plots visualize top-displacement non-target Gaussians rather than all non-target Gaussians,
- no causal claim should be made yet about LBS, Gaussian initialization, learned offsets, or representation failure.
