## Step 1–6 — Spatial localization and joint decomposition

The initial spatial analysis showed that the highest-displacement non-target Gaussians are not distributed uniformly across the body.

### Left ankle z

- left knee = 78.08% of all outside displacement
- right knee = 4.83%
- right ankle = 3.53%

The large ankle signal is therefore dominated by an anatomically adjacent upstream assignment rather than generic global drift.

### Left wrist z

- left elbow = 39.57%
- right wrist = 12.05%
- left shoulder = 11.26%
- right elbow = 9.93%
- right shoulder = 9.12%
- right hand = 5.58%

The strongest component is again upstream on the same kinematic chain, but a substantial contralateral upper-limb component appears.

### Left shoulder z

The total shoulder leakage remains only 0.58%, so its outside-displacement composition must not be compared directly with ankle/wrist totals.

Within that small outside component, left collar and spine3 are major contributors, with additional contralateral upper-limb assignments.

## Step 8 — Confidence robustness

Dominant-joint confidence filtering reduces the apparent outside displacement substantially, showing that semantic assignment ambiguity contributes to the metric.

However, confidence >= 0.9 does not remove the signal. From the saved robustness analysis, a substantial residual remains, especially for wrist motion.

Interpretation: the original dominant-joint leakage metric is partially confounded by blended articulation boundaries, but assignment ambiguity alone is insufficient to explain the residual.

- right elbow = 8.79%

The high-confidence outside-displacement total is much smaller than ankle or wrist, but its composition is dominated by right shoulder/right elbow/right hand/right wrist assignments. Because the absolute shoulder leakage is tiny, this percentage composition should not be interpreted as a more severe failure.

## Step 10 — Signed and magnitude response

Artifact:

- [Signed/magnitude high-confidence response](../../experiments/02-spatial-leakage-localization/analysis/10_signed_magnitude_high_confidence_response.csv)

This is today's strongest mechanistic observation.

### Wrist magnitude sweep

The left-wrist z perturbation was evaluated at ±5°, ±10°, ±20°, and ±30°.

High-confidence outside displacement is approximately proportional to perturbation magnitude:

- ±5° -> ~5.083
- ±10° -> ~10.154
- ±20° -> ~20.23
- ±30° -> ~30.15

The response is also nearly perfectly symmetric between positive and negative rotations.

Even more importantly, the **high-confidence leakage ratio remains nearly constant at ~12.26%** across the entire ±5° to ±30° range.

The anatomical composition is essentially invariant:

- upstream same-side share = ~54.51–54.54%
- contralateral share = ~41.01%

### Ankle sign test

At ±10°:

- outside displacement = 21.8648 vs 21.8704
- high-confidence leakage = 25.523% vs 25.452%
- upstream share = 87.957% for both signs
- contralateral share = 6.723% for both signs

### Shoulder sign test

At ±10°:

- outside displacement = 4.8789 vs 4.8893
- high-confidence leakage = 0.2518% vs 0.2530%
- upstream share = ~12.9%
- contralateral share = ~71.77%

Again, the signed response is nearly symmetric.

## Interpretation after Step 10

The evidence now argues strongly against random numerical noise, a few isolated outliers, or a sign-specific instability.

The wrist response in particular behaves like a deterministic, approximately linear coupling:

1. residual displacement scales almost linearly with perturbation magnitude,
2. positive and negative rotations are nearly mirror-symmetric,
3. leakage ratio is almost constant over a six-fold change in angle magnitude,
4. upstream and contralateral anatomical proportions are nearly invariant.

However, this is **not yet evidence of a HUGS-specific representation failure**. HUGS uses blended SMPL/LBS transforms based on neighboring vertices rather than the single dominant-joint labels used by the diagnostic. A deterministic cross-body response could therefore already be present in the underlying SMPL pose-corrective / skinning machinery.

## Critical caveats

- one NeuMan sequence,
- one frame,
- primary spatial analysis focused on z-axis perturbations,
- dominant-joint assignment is a diagnostic grouping, not ground-truth segmentation,
- HUGS applies blended skinning transforms, not single-joint transforms,
- high-confidence filtering reduces ambiguity but does not reproduce the exact HUGS blending rule,
- no causal claim should yet be made about Gaussian representation error.

## Next decisive experiment

**SMPL-only control vs HUGS Gaussian response.**

Apply the same joint perturbations directly to the underlying SMPL body and measure vertex displacement using comparable anatomical groupings. Then compare:

- signed/magnitude linearity,
- upstream displacement fractions,
- contralateral response,
- normalized displacement distribution.

If the same cross-body and upstream structure appears in SMPL-only deformation, today's residual is largely inherited from the body/skinning model. If HUGS produces substantial additional residual beyond SMPL, that would provide stronger evidence for a representation-specific locality problem.
