# 2026-09-14 — Spatial Leakage Localization

## Research question

Where are the non-target Gaussians responsible for the large distal-joint leakage observed in HUGS Baseline v1, and what mechanism best explains that displacement?

## Starting point

Yesterday's Baseline v1 established a strong articulation-dependent pattern on NeuMan Seattle, frame 0:

- left ankle z: 26.57% leakage
- left wrist z: 11.21% leakage
- left shoulder z: 0.58% leakage

The goal today is not to add another headline number. It is to explain the spatial structure behind those numbers.

## Findings

### Left ankle z

The ankle probe is highly concentrated rather than uniformly distributed across the body.

- left knee accounts for **78.08% of all outside displacement**
- right knee: 4.83%
- right ankle: 3.53%
- every other listed joint contributes less than 3%

This strongly suggests that the large 26.57% ankle leakage is dominated by a specific anatomically adjacent upstream region, especially the left-knee assignment, rather than generic global drift.

The mean dominant-joint confidence for the left-knee group is 0.842, so the effect is not obviously explained by only very low-confidence semantic assignments.

### Left wrist z

The wrist probe is more distributed than the ankle probe.

- left elbow: **39.57% of outside displacement**
- right wrist: 12.05%
- left shoulder: 11.26%
- right elbow: 9.93%
- right shoulder: 9.12%
- right hand: 5.58%

The largest component is again an anatomically adjacent upstream joint, the left elbow. However, a substantial fraction also appears on the contralateral arm. This makes the wrist result qualitatively different from the ankle result and motivates checking whether the contralateral displacement is a real deformation effect, a consequence of Gaussian-to-joint assignment, or a coordinate/pose coupling effect.

### Left shoulder z

The shoulder probe has a very small overall leakage ratio, 0.58%. Its outside-displacement composition should therefore not be compared directly with the ankle or wrist totals.

Within that small outside component:

- left collar: 21.48%
- spine3: 20.32%
- right elbow: 14.12%
- right wrist: 11.16%

The strongest assignments to left collar and spine3 are anatomically plausible neighboring regions for shoulder motion. This supports the interpretation that proximal-joint leakage is both lower in magnitude and more concentrated around expected torso/shoulder-chain boundaries.

## Spatial visualization observations

The highest-displacement non-target Gaussian visualizations were inspected for ankle z, wrist z, and shoulder z.

- **ankle z:** the displacement scale is much larger than for wrist or shoulder, with a small number of extreme high-displacement points and a broader affected structure along the posed body.
- **wrist z:** the highest non-target points form clearer clusters around the upper-limb / torso structure, consistent with the left-elbow and shoulder contributions in the joint breakdown.
- **shoulder z:** the non-target points are comparatively low magnitude and concentrate around adjacent torso / shoulder-chain regions, although isolated distant outliers remain visible.

These plots show only the selected highest-displacement non-target Gaussians and therefore should not be interpreted as a density map of all leakage.

## Step 7 — Concentration analysis

The outside-displacement signal is highly concentrated, but not explained by only a handful of extreme outliers.

For all three probes, the top 0.1% of non-target Gaussians explain only a minority of outside displacement, while the top 5% explain roughly 83–85%. This indicates a structured high-displacement subset involving tens of thousands of Gaussians rather than a few isolated points.

The anatomical grouping also remains strongly structured:

- ankle z: upstream same-side chain ≈ 81.3% of outside displacement,
- wrist z: upstream same-side chain ≈ 52.8%, contralateral upper limb ≈ 37.0%,
- shoulder z: upstream torso attachment ≈ 47.2%, contralateral upper limb ≈ 38.7%.

## Step 8 — Confidence robustness

A confidence-threshold sweep shows that dominant-joint assignment uncertainty explains a substantial portion of the outside-displacement signal, but not all of it.

From the Step 8 robustness plot:

- ankle z retains roughly one quarter of outside displacement at dominant-joint confidence ≥ 0.9,
- wrist z retains roughly two fifths,
- shoulder z retains roughly one quarter.

The exact CSV files saved by the notebook remain authoritative; these percentages are visual readings of the plotted curve and should not replace the saved tables.

This changes the interpretation in an important way. The current leakage metric is partly confounded by semantic assignment ambiguity near blended articulation boundaries. However, the persistence of a non-trivial high-confidence residual, especially for the wrist, means that assignment ambiguity alone does not explain the observed structure.

## Current interpretation

The evidence now supports a mixed explanation rather than a simple global-leakage claim:

1. distal-joint perturbations produce large non-target displacement under the current dominant-joint diagnostic,
2. a large fraction of that displacement is anatomically structured around upstream or cross-body joint groups,
3. low-confidence semantic assignments contribute strongly to the measured leakage,
4. but meaningful displacement remains after strict confidence filtering, particularly for wrist motion,
5. therefore the next step should localize the **high-confidence residual** before attempting a causal claim about HUGS or LBS.

## Important caveats

- one NeuMan sequence,
- one frame,
- z-axis comparison for the primary spatial analysis,
- dominant-joint assignment is a diagnostic grouping rather than ground-truth semantic segmentation,
- HUGS actually uses blended skinning influence rather than a single dominant joint,
- the current 3D plots visualize top-displacement non-target Gaussians rather than all non-target Gaussians,
- no causal claim should yet be made about LBS, Gaussian initialization, or learned offsets.

## Next analysis

Before reproducing HUGS's exact K=6 blended skinning influence, the next low-cost test is to recompute the joint and anatomical breakdown **only for high-confidence Gaussians (confidence ≥ 0.9)**.

If the same ankle→knee and wrist→elbow / contralateral patterns survive, then the residual is spatially structured even where dominant-joint assignment is comparatively unambiguous. If those patterns collapse, the dominant-joint diagnostic is the main source of the apparent locality failure.
