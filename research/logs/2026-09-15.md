# 2026-09-15 - Counterfactual LBS Ablation

## Research question

Does the stable contralateral upper-body response under a controlled left-wrist perturbation collapse when the learned cross-joint left-wrist/hand LBS components are removed while canonical Gaussians, pose transforms, geometry, and all other learned weights are held fixed?

- saved probe: `left_wrist z +10 deg`

- corrected raw NeuMan frame index: `2`
- 472,958 Gaussians
- confidence threshold: `>= 0.9`
- intended joints: left wrist + left hand
- same-side local chain: left collar + left shoulder + left elbow

## Step 14B - independent forward reconstruction

Before running any counterfactual, the HUGS learned-LBS deformation path was reconstructed independently from the saved canonical Gaussians, SMPL pose parameters, learned LBS weights, and the HUGS Vitruvian-to-pose transform convention.

A split-index correction was discovered during validation. `probe frame_index=0` corresponds to raw NeuMan frame **2**, not raw frame 22. The HUGS dataset code stores the third return value of `get_data_splits()` as `val_split`, so the relevant raw-frame sequence is the test-list portion `[2, 7, 12, 17]`.

At raw frame 2, reconstruction against the saved HUGS probe was essentially exact:

## Steps 14C-14D - mask reconciliation

The first counterfactual pass produced 32,603 high-confidence contralateral Gaussians, whereas Step 13E had reported 33,072. The difference was traced exactly to omission of the right-collar joint from the Step 14C mask:

```text
right-collar high-confidence count: 469
32603 + 469 = 33072
```

The same audit showed that the same-side local-chain definition should include the left collar. Step 14E therefore restored the exact Step 13E upper-body definitions rather than silently changing the mask.

## Step 14E - corrected causal counterfactual

Three conditions were compared under the same left-wrist z +10 degree perturbation:

1. original learned HUGS LBS,
2. reconstructed SMPL-derived K=6 target LBS,
3. learned LBS with only the left-wrist and left-hand channels zeroed on the exact high-confidence contralateral subset, followed by row renormalization.

No weights outside the 33,072-Gaussian contralateral subset were changed.

## Interpretation

The corrected counterfactual provides direct causal evidence **within this fixed Seattle/raw-frame-2 diagnostic** that the learned cross-joint left-wrist/hand LBS component is necessary for the observed high-confidence contralateral upper-body displacement.

The result is stronger when read together with the prior controls:

1. the SMPL-derived K=6 target assigns essentially no direct left-wrist/hand support to these contralateral Gaussians,
2. the HUGS learned field introduces small cross-joint wrist/hand weights while preserving right-upper-body dominant assignment,
3. those learned weights are strongly associated with displacement,
4. replacing the learned field with K=6 reduces contralateral displacement by ~99.82%,
5. selectively removing only the learned wrist/hand channels from the contralateral subset eliminates the measured contralateral response under this perturbation.

The zero residual under selective ablation should not be overinterpreted as a general property of HUGS. In an LBS model, removing the channels whose transforms change under this perturbation removes that perturbation pathway by construction. The scientific contribution here is the demonstrated existence and quantitative importance of those unexpected learned cross-joint channels relative to the K=6 target and SMPL-only control.

## Defensible claim

> In the Seattle raw-frame-2 left-wrist-z diagnostic, the pretrained HUGS learned deformation field introduces cross-joint wrist/hand LBS components that causally mediate the amplified contralateral upper-body response relative to the SMPL-derived K=6 target.

Do **not** generalize this yet to all HUGS sequences, frames, joints, or Gaussian-human methods.
