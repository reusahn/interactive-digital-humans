## Chain checks

All three checkpoints satisfy:

- K6 positive-support sets are nested as the tested branch expands wrist -> elbow -> shoulder.
- learned positive-support sets are nested.
- K6 positive-support fraction is nondecreasing wrist -> elbow -> shoulder.
- learned-positive/K6-zero topology mismatch is nonincreasing wrist -> elbow -> shoulder.

The major structural transition occurs at the shoulder. K6 support is present on only about `0.20%` to `0.63%` of contralateral rows for wrist/elbow, then rises to about `8.55%` to `26.15%` at shoulder.

## Interpretation

This topology pattern is strongly aligned with the previously frozen deformation results:

- wrist and elbow: K6 support is nearly absent on contralateral rows and K6 replacement reduces learned contralateral response by at least about `99.7%`.
- shoulder: K6 descendant support becomes much more available, and the strong learned-vs-K6 suppression no longer generalizes.
- Parkinglot shoulder is the clearest reversal: aggregate K6 shoulder support exceeds learned support and K6 shoulder displacement also exceeds learned displacement.

Together with Step 18C2, which showed that matching per-Gaussian shoulder branch mass removes most of the learned-vs-K6 spatial-field gap, the most specific bounded exploratory explanation is **branch-support topology plus per-row support magnitude**, not kinematic depth by itself.

This still does not establish kinematic depth as an independent causal variable. The branches are nested by construction and the same LBS support participates mechanically in the deformation response.
