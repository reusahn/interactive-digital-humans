## Interpretation

The predeclared third-joint generalization criterion fails at frame 2 in all three independently pretrained checkpoints. This is a genuine scientific result, not a numerical-regression blocker.

The strongest failed component is the `>=95%` K6-reduction criterion:

- Seattle: `38.89%`
- Parkinglot: `-49.15%`
- Jogging: `59.77%`

Therefore the wrist/elbow finding that SMPL K6 almost eliminates contralateral response does **not** generalize to the shoulder under the frozen protocol. Parkinglot is especially informative because K6 produces more shoulder contralateral displacement than the learned weights.

At the same time, selective removal of the shoulder descendant branch from learned weights eliminates contralateral response exactly in all three checkpoints. This means the response is still mediated through weights on the transforms that actually change under the shoulder perturbation. However, that fact alone does not establish learned-weight amplification relative to K6, because K6 already carries substantial shoulder-branch influence.

The removed-mass/displacement relationship remains strong in Seattle and Parkinglot but falls just below the predeclared threshold in Jogging (`0.8946`).

A plausible interpretation to test, not yet a conclusion, is kinematic-depth dependence: learned-vs-SMPL locality amplification may be much stronger for distal wrist/elbow perturbations than for the more proximal shoulder, where standard SMPL skinning itself distributes substantial influence through the arm branch.

No threshold, joint, branch, mask, angle, axis, or pose schedule is changed after seeing this result.

## Consequence for the protocol

The frame-2 result already means the predeclared universal third-joint pass criterion cannot be recovered by later poses. The remaining predeclared pose schedule should still be run, but only to characterize the robustness and pose dependence of this failure, not to rescue or redefine the shoulder hypothesis.
