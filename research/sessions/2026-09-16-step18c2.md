## Question

How much of the learned-vs-K6 shoulder displacement-field difference can be explained by total shoulder-descendant branch-support magnitude alone?

Counterfactual definition on frozen contralateral rows:

- preserve learned within-branch composition for branch `[16,18,20,22]`
- replace learned total branch mass with the corresponding K6 total branch mass
- preserve learned non-branch proportions while rescaling to maintain row sum
- fallback to K6 composition only if learned branch/non-branch composition is undefined

All tested rows used pure learned composition. Fallback count was zero in every checkpoint.

## Scientific interpretation

The Step 18C2 counterfactual provides strong exploratory evidence that **total shoulder-descendant branch-support magnitude is a dominant driver of the shoulder learned-vs-K6 displacement-field difference**.

Across all three checkpoints, replacing only total learned branch mass with K6 branch mass while retaining learned within-branch composition removes a large fraction of the original field discrepancy. Median MAE-gap reduction is approximately:

```text
Seattle:    94.50%
Parkinglot: 83.85%
Jogging:    93.52%
```

Counterfactual-vs-K6 spatial correlation is very high for most poses.

However, the result is not a complete mechanistic proof:

- residual field error remains after mass matching
- some poses show substantially lower RMSE-gap reduction or field correlation
- aggregate displacement sums can overshoot K6 even when the spatial field moves much closer to K6
- within-branch joint composition and pose-dependent geometry remain plausible residual factors

The correct bounded interpretation is therefore:

> Shoulder learned-vs-K6 behavior is largely governed by how much total weight mass each representation assigns to the perturbed shoulder-descendant branch. Branch mass is a dominant explanatory factor, but not the sole determinant of the resulting displacement field.

This does not establish kinematic depth as the underlying cause of the wrist/elbow/shoulder pattern.
