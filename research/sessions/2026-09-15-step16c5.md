## Interpretation

The causal mechanism is robust to perturbation axis and sign in Parkinglot, but the response magnitude is anisotropic.

The `x`-axis perturbation produces a much smaller learned contralateral response (`~0.315-0.317`, about `0.33%` of high-confidence displacement) than `y` or `z` (`~5.415-5.417`, about `3.4-3.8%` of high-confidence displacement). Therefore the correct conclusion is not axis-invariant magnitude. Instead:

- all three tested axes show learned cross-joint amplification relative to the K=6 target,
- K=6 replacement removes at least 98.80% of the contralateral response,
- selective removal of learned wrist/hand channels removes the tested contralateral response completely for every axis/sign,
- removed learned wrist/hand mass remains strongly associated with displacement reduction for every condition,
- perturbation sign has little effect on magnitude.

This substantially reduces the concern that the causal finding is a peculiarity of the originally selected positive-z perturbation.

## Strongest current claim

The learned cross-joint left-wrist/hand LBS mechanism is causally observed and pose-stable in two independently pretrained HUGS NeuMan checkpoints, Seattle and Parkinglot. In Parkinglot it is also robust across left-wrist x/y/z rotations and both perturbation signs, although effect magnitude is strongly axis-dependent.

Do not call the effect isotropic and do not generalize yet to all HUGS checkpoints, joints, or Gaussian-human methods.
