## Interpretation

The exploratory evidence strongly supports shoulder-descendant branch support as a major explanatory factor for the joint-dependent learned-vs-K6 shoulder behavior.

- The checkpoint-level direction of aggregate branch support matches the majority learned-vs-K6 displacement direction in all three independently pretrained checkpoints.
- Per-Gaussian differences in branch support are strongly correlated with per-Gaussian learned-minus-K6 displacement differences across all tested poses, with correlations ranging from about `0.802` to `0.982`.
- Parkinglot is particularly informative: K6 carries more shoulder-branch support than learned weights and also produces more contralateral displacement in all four poses.

However, branch support amount is not a complete explanation.

- Seattle frame 7 reverses the aggregate support direction despite a high per-Gaussian correlation (`0.9362`).
- Displacement ratios vary substantially by pose even though branch weights are pose-independent.
- Jogging has a learned/K6 support ratio of about `5.56`, while displacement ratios vary only from about `1.73` to `4.24`.

Therefore pose geometry and/or within-branch joint composition still modulate how branch support is converted into displacement. The result does not establish kinematic depth as a causal mechanism.
