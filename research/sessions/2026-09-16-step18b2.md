## Scientific interpretation

The shoulder result is a genuine negative generalization result under the predeclared criterion.

1. The wrist/elbow finding that replacing learned LBS with subject-specific SMPL K6 removes at least roughly 99.7% of contralateral response does **not** generalize to the proximal shoulder. Shoulder K6 reduction fails in all 18 frozen pose diagnostics.
2. Parkinglot is qualitatively reversed across all four poses: K6 creates more contralateral displacement than learned LBS, with reductions from about `-44.7%` to `-77.7%`.
3. Seattle is mixed, including one negative K6 reduction. Jogging consistently shows learned > K6, but only by roughly `42.3%` to `76.4%`, far below the predeclared `95%` threshold.
4. Selective learned-branch ablation eliminates the tested contralateral response in all 18 shoulder poses. Therefore the response remains causally mediated through the changed shoulder-descendant branch under this construction.
5. Removed branch mass remains strongly associated with displacement reduction in Seattle and Parkinglot, but the correlation is less stable in Jogging and falls below the frozen `0.90` criterion in 4 of 10 poses.

The strongest bounded conclusion is therefore joint-dependent: the learned-LBS versus SMPL-K6 amplification effect is robust for the tested distal wrist and elbow perturbations, but not universal across the more proximal shoulder perturbation. The shoulder results do not negate branch-mediated causality, but they reject a simple joint-invariant claim that K6 nearly abolishes contralateral response everywhere along the arm chain.

A kinematic-depth explanation is plausible but remains a hypothesis, not an established mechanism.
